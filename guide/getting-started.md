# Getting started

Lekto is an immersive reader for language learners. It runs on Android and on the
desktop, and it works entirely offline: there is no account to create and no Lekto
server to reach. The desktop installers, and the one accessibility limitation that
comes with the desktop, are on [Lekto on the desktop](/desktop).

## First launch

The first time you open Lekto it creates a **Vault** for you: the private,
on-device store that holds everything you author or import. You do not need to
sign in, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

It then asks one question: **Which language would you like to read first?** Choose a
language — the row stays selected and the **Continue** button below becomes
available — then **Continue** opens a short **Sample** of it in the reader: first with no colour,
then the same page coloured by a level, with a few words explained under it. Under
the language list, a card names the word-difficulty table and offline dictionary
that choice downloads in the background, with **Stop** while it runs, **Resume**
after a stop, or **Retry** when a connection failure stopped it; a data format this
version cannot read, or a language that is not published yet, says so instead. Tap
any word the sample
explains and the lookup sheet opens; choosing a level saves the word to your
**Vocabulary** and recolours it on the page. Then the same page sits above the six
CEFR bands: A1 is chosen to begin with, so the page tells the truth; pick another
band and the page repaints. **Start reading** saves that level. The sample is
waiting on the Library when you finish.

You can leave the guide at any time with **Skip** — or **Escape** on a computer,
or the system Back gesture on Android. Every screen after the first also has a back
chevron at the head of the bar. The sample stays on the shelf, and a line there
reminds you where its reading level is set. The guide records only that you finished
it, so an
unfinished guide starts over next time; you can replay it later from
**Settings → Application → Guide**, and a replay asks the language question again.

The app's own language is a separate thing. When Lekto ships the language your
device is set to (English or French), it starts in that language; otherwise it
starts in English. That is the **app language** — the language of the app's own
texts — and you can change it any time in **Settings → App language**. The
language your translations appear in is a separate choice, the **Translation
language**, made on the **Languages** surface. See [Settings and FAQ](/settings-and-faq).

If you already use Lekto on another device, you can turn on sync in Settings to
bring your books, reading progress and vocabulary here. See
[Vault and sync](/vault-and-sync).

## The shape of the app

Lekto has three sections, and you move between them from the bar at the bottom of
the screen — a column at the left on a desktop:

- The **Library** lists the books you have imported. It is where the app opens.
- **Vocabulary** holds the words you have saved.
- **Settings** holds everything else: appearance, languages, the provider, the Vault.

**Import** is an action in the Library, not a fourth section: it brings a book
original into the Vault so it appears in the Library.

A reading session opens a book and colours its Word tokens by your **Mastery
level**. It leaves the three sections and fills the screen; the arrow at its top
left brings you back to the Library. **Back** — the system back gesture on Android
— walks the way you came: it returns from Settings to the section you were in, and
only leaves the app from the Library.

A tap on a word opens the lookup panel, so you can see a translation, hear its
**Pronunciation**, and save it.
