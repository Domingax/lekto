# Word lookup

Tap a **Word token** while reading to open the lookup panel for that word.

## What the panel shows

The panel rises from the bottom of the page and never covers more than a little
under half of it. The page slides up under the panel just enough to keep the word
you touched in view, and that word stays highlighted. The page does not change:
it is not split again, and the top of it is hidden for as long as the panel is
open.

On a desktop with a wide window the panel is not raised over the page at all: it
takes the reader's right-hand column, and the page keeps its width. Its level name
stands above the chips there, because five chips leave no room for the name beside
them. Close it and the column returns to the chapter list. See
[Reading](/reading#on-a-desktop).

- The word in the reading serif, with its part of speech and pronunciation under
  it.
- The word's **Lemma** when the form you touched is an inflection, so you can see
  the dictionary form that mastery and vocabulary hang off.
- Its senses, numbered, each with a translation when an offline dictionary pack is
  installed for your **Translation language**.
- A speaker icon that plays the word's **Pronunciation**, and a close icon that
  closes the panel.
- The word's **Mastery level**, and the external references at the bottom.

## Setting the level saves the word

The level is the save. Tap one of the five level chips — 0 unknown to 4 known —
and Lekto stores the **Vocabulary entry** at that level, with the **Context
sentence** it appeared in and a translation to fall back on. One tap, one
intention.

The current level's name stands beside the chips, so the numbers never need a
legend. Tapping another chip changes the level of a word you already saved, and
the word's colour in the page follows at once. There is no separate Save button.

See [Vocabulary](/vocabulary) for the list of saved words.

## Dictionary shortcuts

When you want a fuller answer than the offline pack gives, the panel's footer is a
single muted line of **Dictionary shortcuts**: the word's page on an external
**Dictionary source** such as WordReference, Reverso, Linguee or Google Translate.
The shortcuts follow the pair the reader chose: the book's language into your
**Translation language**.

A dictionary shortcut is an ordinary outbound link. Lekto never embeds or scrapes
the source; opening the shortcut hands the page to your device's own browser.

## Closing the panel

Close the panel with its close icon, or by tapping the page outside it. A tap
outside the panel only closes it: it does not look up the word under your finger
or turn the page.
