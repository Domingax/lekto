# Word lookup

Tap a **Word token** while reading to open the lookup panel for that word.

## What the panel shows

The panel rises from the bottom of the page and takes only as much room as its
answer needs, up to three fifths of the screen. A word with a single sense opens
a short panel; a long answer rises to that limit and scrolls inside it. The page
slides up under the panel just enough to keep the word you touched in view, and
that word stays highlighted. The page does not change: it is not split again, and
the top of it is hidden for as long as the panel is open.

On a desktop with a wide window the panel is not raised over the page at all: it
takes the reader's right-hand column, and the page keeps its width. The line that
states whether the word is saved stands above the chips there, because five chips
leave no room for it beside them. Close it and the column returns to the chapter
list. See [Reading](/reading#on-a-desktop).

- The word in the reading serif, with its part of speech and pronunciation under
  it.
- The word's **Lemma** when the form you touched is an inflection, so you can see
  the dictionary form that mastery and vocabulary hang off.
- Its senses, numbered, each with a translation when an offline dictionary pack is
  installed for your **Translation language**.
- A speaker icon that plays the word's **Pronunciation**, and a close icon that
  closes the panel.
- The word's **Mastery level**, and the external references at the end of the
  panel's body.

## Setting the level saves the word

The level is the save. Tap one of the five level chips — 0 unknown to 4 known —
and Lekto stores the **Vocabulary entry** at that level, with the **Context
sentence** it appeared in and a translation to fall back on. One tap, one
intention.

The footer states the word's state, never an intention: it reads `Not saved`
before you save, or `Likely Recognized` when the level Lekto derived from the word
and your **Reader level** suggests one. Once the word is stored it reads `Saved ·
Recognized` and the saved level's chip carries a ring, so a suggestion is never
mistaken for a fact and the numbers never need a legend. Tapping another chip
changes the level of a word you already saved, and the word's colour in the page
follows at once. If the vault cannot take the write, the footer says `Couldn't
save` and the word stays unsaved. There is no separate Save button.

See [Vocabulary](/vocabulary) for the list of saved words.

## Dictionary shortcuts

When you want a fuller answer than the offline pack gives, the end of the panel's
body is a single muted line of **Dictionary shortcuts**: the word's page on an
external **Dictionary source** such as WordReference, Reverso, Linguee or Google
Translate. The shortcuts follow the pair the reader chose: the book's language
into your **Translation language**. They scroll with the answer, so a long line
never takes room from the definition above it.

A dictionary shortcut is an ordinary outbound link. Lekto never embeds or scrapes
the source; opening the shortcut hands the page to your device's own browser.

## When the offline dictionary is missing

If no **Dictionary pack** is installed for the pair, the panel says so and still
lets you set the word's level. It also offers one quiet action, `Install the
offline dictionary`, which opens Settings ▸ Languages and leaves the book behind
— the pack is downloaded there, never inside the reader. Back from Settings
returns you to the Library.

## Closing the panel

Close the panel with its close icon, by dragging its handle down, or by tapping
the page outside it. A tap outside the panel only closes it: it does not look up
the word under your finger or turn the page.
